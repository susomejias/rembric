## Why

`ABSTENTION_FLOOR` has no usable dynamic range, and no value of it can ever be calibrated — the quantity it is scored against is not a relevance measure.

`normalizeLexicalScore` is `1/(1+exp(bm25))`, and FTS5 clamps a non-positive IDF to `1e-6`, so `bm25` is always `≤ ~-1e-6` and the normalised value is **always ≥ 0.5**. Measured this week on one machine against a 200-row corpus: a term appearing in 3/200 rows scores **0.980**; in 150/200 rows, **0.5000002**; in 200/200 rows, **0.5000003**. The curve saturates almost immediately — bm25 `-5` → 0.993, `-10` → 0.99995, `-20` → 0.9999999979. `hybridSearch` takes `bestScore = Math.max(lexical[0].score, dense[0].score)` and `sanitizeFtsQuery` ORs every token, so the lexical branch is almost never empty. Therefore **any floor ≤ 0.5 can never fire, and any floor above 0.5 is an IDF cliff rather than a relevance gate.** bm25 magnitude also grows with corpus size and term IDF, so the space is not even stable between two installations. This is the same defect this repo already fixed once: `fix-retrieval-ranking-math` removed an absolute bm25 gate from save-time candidate detection on the grounds that "BM25 is unbounded and corpus-relative, so _no_ absolute floor on it is stable" — and replaced it with bounded token containment. The identical gate was left standing on the search path.

The gap ratio is in no better shape, for a second structural reason. It is evaluated post-fusion and post-boost over RRF scores, comparing consecutive pairs. RRF scores are a function of rank position only, and the arithmetic of `RANK_CONSTANT = 60` fixes the consecutive ratios: **within a branch-membership class the ratio is 0.984 at rank 1 rising to 0.996 by rank 200, and at the both-branches → single-branch boundary it is exactly 0.500** (0.508 at the extremes of realistic rank pairs). So `GAP_RATIO_THRESHOLD` is a three-valued knob: `≤ 0.5` never fires, `0.5 < r < 0.98` degenerates into "return only rows both branches found", `≥ 0.98` truncates at rank 2. The post-fusion boost then multiplies each score by `[0.9, 1.35]` — a 1.5× spread that smears 0.984 across `[0.656, 1.476]` and overlaps the 0.500 cliff, so post-boost the two regimes are not separable at all.

The result is two knobs, in two different score spaces, both shipping `null`, neither calibratable. And the harness that `improve-recall-relevance` made the precondition for enabling them cannot yet decide the value: `pnpm run eval` reports `abstainFP=1.00` on all three retrievers **because nothing ever abstains**, there are only 2 abstention queries (so the metric has three attainable values), and over-abstention — a gold-bearing query that wrongly returns nothing — is not measured at all, only folded into recall where it is indistinguishable from a wrong answer.

## What Changes

- **Score both gates on a rank-invariant relevance level, not on branch-native scores.** Each row in the gate window gets `level ∈ [0,1]` = `max(lexical coverage, dense cosine)`, where lexical coverage is the existing `tokenContainment` — the fraction of the query's distinct tokens present in the row's title+content, bounded by construction, corpus-size independent, and already the shipped save-time lexical measure. **Rejected: min-max or rank-percentile over the branch's own rank window.** Both map the leader to a constant (1.0) by construction, so no absolute floor could ever fire on them; a window-relative quantity encodes _shape_, never _level_, and a floor needs a level. **Rejected: per-branch floors as two independent knobs** — dropping a whole branch's candidates changes what fusion sees, so the two thresholds interact and neither can be swept alone; one number in one place is calibratable, two are not. Per-branch levels are still _reported_ by the sweep, so a follow-up can split them on evidence.
- **One evaluation point for both gates: immediately after fusion, before the ranking boost.** The floor is absolute on the highest level in the gate window; the relative filter drops rows whose level is below `ratio × leaderLevel`. Pre-boost, because the boost is a ranking multiplier over recency and confirmation count — it is not a relevance measure, and letting it move a relevance gate is how a fresh, confirmed, irrelevant row survives an abstention check. **Rejected: floor pre-fusion, gap post-boost (today's split).** It is exactly the arrangement `reconcile-specs-with-shipped-behaviour` had to write two paragraphs of spec to explain, and its stated reason — RRF scores measure list shape rather than match quality — is answered by not scoring either gate on RRF at all.
- **The tail filter becomes a leader-relative filter, which is what `memory/spec.md` already says.** The spec specifies the gap "relative to that best score"; the code compares consecutive pairs. This change makes the code match the spec rather than the reverse, on two grounds. First, the consecutive form cannot work over any quantity that decays gradually — a 0.9/0.8/0.7/0.6/0.5/0.4/0.3 tail passes every consecutive test at `r = 0.5` and returns a row at 33% of the leader. Second, over a non-monotone level sequence in fused order, truncate-at-first-offender is order-dependent and discards strictly better rows that happen to sit behind a weak one; a per-row test against the leader is order-independent and idempotent. **This corrects, rather than adopts, the justification `reconcile-specs-with-shipped-behaviour` recorded for the consecutive form** ("a per-best test would keep a long flat tail whose every member is individually far from the leader") — that is inverted: a flat tail far below the leader is precisely what a per-best test cuts and a consecutive test keeps.
- **Both gates still ship `null`, and the spec's guarantee becomes a calibration bar rather than an assertion about a constant's current value.** `improve-recall-relevance` recorded that abstention is unshippable without the harness; that stands. The requirement is rewritten to say _what a commit enabling a gate must demonstrate_ — a committed sweep across a grid, zero over-abstention on gold-bearing queries, `abstainFP` at or under its cap, and the chosen value sitting in the interior of a plateau at least two grid steps wide rather than on a cliff edge. Whether this change lands the gates enabled is then an outcome of the sweep, recorded in `tasks.md`, not a promise made in advance.
- **The harness gets the two error axes a floor needs, and a sweep target.** `overAbstentionRate` (gold-bearing queries returning nothing) is added alongside `abstentionFalsePositiveRate`, both gated in `checkFloors` as **caps** (lower is better) rather than floors, because this is the change that gives them meaning. The abstention query set grows from 2 to at least 8 so the metric has resolution, adversarially drawn so the OR-ed lexical branch has vocabulary to latch onto. `pnpm run eval --sweep-abstention` prints the `(floor, ratio) → recall / abstainFP / overAbstention / tokens` grid so the chosen number is reproducible from a committed command instead of asserted.
- **Zero extra work on the hot path while the gates are disabled.** The level computation needs the gate window's title+content, so it runs only when at least one gate is non-`null`. With both `null` — the shipped state — `memory.search` issues the same queries and returns byte-identical results.

Adjacent, deliberately **not** in scope: `DIVERSITY_CAP` also ships `null` (`57b39a2`), but for an unrelated reason — it is applied to the whole fused pool before the page slice, which measurably swaps most of page 1 for noise, and the eval corpus cannot see it because every corpus row has `session_id = NULL`. Its re-enablement needs a session-labelled eval fixture and belongs in its own change.

Also cross-referenced, not duplicated: `retrieval-evaluation/spec.md` claims a floor per metric while `checkFloors` gates three of five — `avgTokensReturned` is computed, printed and discarded. That is `reconcile-specs-with-shipped-behaviour` task 5.12 / A12. This change gates the two abstention metrics because it is the change that makes them discriminate; `avgTokensReturned` stays with that change. `writeBaseline`'s missing ratchet (same change, task 8.8) is a known hazard for the baseline regeneration here and is called out in `tasks.md` rather than fixed.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

(none)

### Modified Capabilities

- `memory`: the abstention requirement — what quantity the two gates read, the single evaluation point, the leader-relative (not consecutive) filter semantics, and the calibration bar a commit must meet to enable either gate. The retrieval-constants requirement — the renamed gap constant and the new gate-window constant, and the replacement of "these two ship disabled" with the condition under which they may stop being disabled.
- `retrieval-evaluation`: abstention SHALL be scored on two error axes rather than one, both gated as caps; the abstention query set SHALL have enough members to resolve a threshold; the harness SHALL be able to emit a calibration sweep.
