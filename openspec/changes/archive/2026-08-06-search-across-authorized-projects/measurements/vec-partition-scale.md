# Measurement — what a widened `memory.search` costs at production scale

**This is the production-scale half of the phase-2 question.** The
capability-and-ordering half is `vec-partition-capability.md`, measured at propose time on a
synthetic in-memory fixture; its §5 lists six things it does not establish, and this document closes
them. Read §5 and §8 here before quoting anything from it.

Status: **measured**. Every figure below was executed on this machine on 2026-08-05 and comes from a
the committed harness below. Nothing is extrapolated, and no table is retyped — every one is
generated by `scale-summarize.mjs` from those files.

---

## 0. The three instruments, named once, and which table uses which

`CLAUDE.md` forbids presenting an isolated statement's timing and an end-to-end operation's timing as
one series — "a 39× statement speedup was a 2.5× user-facing one, and the ratio came from silently
switching instruments between rows". There are three instruments here and every table names its own.

|        | instrument             | what it measures                                                                                                                                                         | what it is NOT                                                 |
| ------ | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **I1** | **ISOLATED STATEMENT** | one prepared statement, through the same drizzle handle the repositories use                                                                                             | anything a user waits on                                       |
| **I2** | **SHIPPED END-TO-END** | one `await MemoryService.searchWithAbstention({ query }, projectScope(id))` on the REAL repositories — embedding, FTS, kNN, RRF, term statistics, gate, boost, hydration | the widening; it is today's narrow search and the control      |
| **I3** | **SHADOW END-TO-END**  | the same service and the same `hybridSearch`, with exactly three repository reads overlaid in the `IN (…)` form phase 4 will ship                                        | the shipped widening — phase 4 has not been written (see §8.1) |

**I2 is the number a user waits on**, and it is the one the release note must quote. I1 exists to
attribute I2, and the two never share a table. I2 and I3 are the same instrument family and are
directly comparable to each other and to `narrow-path-regression.md`'s figures, which use I2.

**I3 is a prototype, and its fidelity is asserted rather than assumed.** Its one-project arm
(`shadow-1`) returned **byte-identical ids to I2 on all 1 440 compared pages, all of them non-empty**,
at every magnitude and from both homes. A second arm, `overlay-passthrough`, puts the SHIPPED reads
behind the same `Proxy`, so the harness can subtract its own cost: it reads **1.00 / 0.93 / 0.99 /
1.01 / 1.03 / 1.00** of `shipped-narrow` across the six corpus/home pairs, i.e. free.

Conditions: 8 vCPU, 15 GB RAM, Node 22.23.1, `better-sqlite3@12.11.1`, SQLite 3.53.2,
`sqlite-vec@0.1.9`, corpora on real disk (not the tmpfs `/tmp`), no dev stack running. **A second agent
was working on the same host throughout**, so absolute milliseconds carry more noise than
`narrow-path-regression.md`'s did; every comparison below is therefore made BETWEEN ARMS INSIDE ONE
PROCESS, interleaved with the arm order rotated per query, which is what removes it (§6 of that
document: "an arm measured at a different time is not comparable to one measured now, however many
repeats each has").

Repeats are reported individually and reduced with the **median**, never the mean — averaging is what
hid the bimodal cell corrected in `vec-partition-capability.md` §4.

## 2. The corpus, and the one property that changes the answer

Built by `apps/server/src/scripts/seed-volumetric.ts` with the new `--skew` flag, at seed `20260805`:

```
--db <root>/skew-1000  --memories 1000  --sessions 20   --seed 20260805 --skew
--db <root>/skew-20000 --memories 20000 --sessions 400  --seed 20260805 --skew
--db <root>/skew-50000 --memories 50000 --sessions 1000 --seed 20260805 --skew
```

`--skew` splits the memory axis 60 / 20 / 10 / 5 / 3 / 2 % over the six projects instead of evenly, so
`vol-0` dominates and `vol-shared` is the thinnest. Slots are assigned by a smooth weighted
round-robin over a 100-long block, so **each project's rows span the whole `created_at` window** — a
cumulative-threshold split would have given the dominant project the oldest rows and the recency term
of the ranking boost would have read the skew as an age difference. Asserted by test
(`seed-volumetric.test.ts`, "spreads every project across the whole created_at span").

| magnitude | `vol-0` | `vol-1` | `vol-2` | `vol-3` | `vol-4` | `vol-shared` | `default` |
| --------: | ------: | ------: | ------: | ------: | ------: | -----------: | --------: |
|     1 000 |     600 |     200 |     100 |      50 |      30 |           20 |         0 |
|    20 000 |  12 000 |   4 000 |   2 000 |   1 000 |     600 |          400 |         0 |
|    50 000 |  30 000 |  10 000 |   5 000 |   2 500 |   1 500 |        1 000 |         0 |

**The skew is not decoration; it is most of the difference between this document and
`vec-partition-capability.md` §4.** That measurement split 8 partitions evenly, so widening to all of
them multiplied the rows scanned by 8 and cost ≈8×. Here the home project already holds 60% of the
corpus, so widening to everything multiplies the rows scanned by ≈1.67 — and §3 shows the end-to-end
cost is smaller still.

**Two homes are measured, and they bound the answer.** `vol-0` is the ordinary case (you widen from
the project you work in). `vol-shared` is the worst case (you widen from a project holding 2% of the
corpus, so the rows you add dwarf the rows you had).

The phase-0 corpora under `narrow-*` were **not rebuilt and not touched**; these `skew-*` corpora are
built beside them. `seed-volumetric` reproduces content from its seed but not ids (`ulid()` draws from
`Math.random()`), so a rebuild is a different corpus for ranking purposes — task 0.1's deviation note
records the measurement.

**Inherited caveat, restated because it bounds every claim here:** the corpus vectors are
deterministic pseudo-random unit vectors, **not embeddings**, and the harnesses' query embedder is of
the same family. **No retrieval-quality, ranking, fusion or abstention claim may be drawn from any run
in this document.** What is claimed is wall-clock, row counts, project-of-origin counts and candidate
pool composition, none of which depends on vector semantics.

## 3. Non-vacuity — asserted by the harnesses, at every magnitude

A latency comparison over two empty result sets proves nothing. Every harness **exits non-zero** on any
of: a timed query returning zero rows on any arm; a one-project arm returning a foreign row; a widened
arm whose results all came from one project; the `shadow-1` ids diverging from `shipped-narrow`; the
three `IN` forms disagreeing on rows; the `textByIds` arms returning different row counts.

Across all 36 end-to-end runs every arm returned a full page (`DEFAULT_SEARCH_LIMIT = 8`) on all 40
timed queries — 320 rows per arm per run — and every widened arm returned rows from at least two
projects. Every narrow arm returned **0** foreign-project rows; that is the same quantity phase 3 will
gate as `foreignScopeRate`.

Read off it, in order of what each decides:

- **The number the release note quotes is 1.35×, not 8×.** Widening from the project you work in to
  every authorized project costs **1.32–1.35× end to end** at all three magnitudes — 66.85 → 90.55 ms
  at 50 000 memories. The ≈8× in `vec-partition-capability.md` §4 is a true statement about an
  isolated dense statement over eight EQUAL partitions, and quoting it as a user-facing cost would
  repeat the mistake `CLAUDE.md` names. Two things separate them, and both are measured here: the
  corpus is skewed (§2) and the end-to-end call pays embedding, FTS, fusion, term statistics, the
  relevance gate, the boost and hydration, none of which scale with the number of projects.
- **From a small project the cost is real: up to 5.0×.** `vol-shared` holds 2% of the corpus, so
  widening multiplies the rows it scans by ≈50. Even so the absolute figure stays modest (2.00 →
  10.00 ms at 1 000; 33.09 → 84.27 ms at 50 000), and the ratio falls sharply as the corpus grows
  (5.00 → 2.39 → 2.55) because the fixed end-to-end costs stop dominating the narrow arm.
- **The per-turn cost the description-level restraint requirement (D15) is justified by is

  unasked, is still a tax — but the artifact it cites should be this table.
- **Widening never returns fewer rows and never returns more.** Every arm returned 320 rows over 40
  queries. What changes is where they come from.

## 5. Task 2.5 — the single-element `IN`, the shape every EXISTING search takes

This is the cost question that touches all of today's traffic rather than only the new feature: if the
implementation carries one query shape (design D6), then every non-widened search becomes
`partition_key IN (?)` with one element. §3 of `vec-partition-capability.md` proved the two forms
return identical ROWS on a 4-vector corpus; §4 timed only `= ?`. `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN` cannot
adjudicate — both forms print the identical opaque vtable index string, re-confirmed here (§6).

### I1 ISOLATED STATEMENT — each scoped read, `IN (one element)` against `= ?`

Ratios are the median of six per-process p50s; `<1.00` means the `IN` form is faster.

| read                                    | 1 000 `vol-0` | 1 000 `vol-shared` | 20 000 `vol-0` | 20 000 `vol-shared` | 50 000 `vol-0` | 50 000 `vol-shared` |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------: | -----------------: | -------------: | ------------------: | -------------: | ------------------: |
| dense `IN(1)` bound ÷ `= ?`             |          0.96 |               0.90 |           0.98 |                0.88 |           0.98 |                0.86 |
| dense `IN(1)` literal ÷ `= ?`           |          0.93 |               0.79 |           0.98 |                0.83 |           0.99 |                0.78 |
| dense `IN(1)` `json_each` ÷ `= ?`       |          0.90 |               0.77 |           0.98 |                0.79 |           0.99 |                0.76 |
| lexical `IN(1)` `json_each` ÷ `= ?`     |          0.69 |               0.57 |           1.05 |                1.00 |           1.04 |                1.01 |
| `textByIds` `IN(1)` `json_each` ÷ `= ?` |          0.76 |               0.67 |           0.54 |                0.55 |           0.51 |                0.51 |

Over the 108 individual dense comparisons (3 forms × 36 runs) the `IN` form was **slower in 2**, with a
median ratio of **0.895** and a worst case of **1.051**. `textByIds` is roughly **half** the cost as an
`IN`. The lexical read is parity at the magnitudes where it costs anything.

### I2 against I3 — the same question end to end

|                                                                   | median ratio | range         | slower in |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | -----------: | ------------- | --------: |
| `overlay-passthrough` ÷ `shipped-narrow` (the harness's own cost) |        1.000 | 0.902 – 1.046 |  17 of 36 |
| `shadow-1` ÷ `shipped-narrow`                                     |        1.016 | 0.854 – 1.065 |  24 of 36 |
| `shadow-1` ÷ `overlay-passthrough`                                |        1.014 | 0.886 – 1.049 |  26 of 36 |

**Decision — the single query shape stands; D6 needs no change.** Justification, in the order it
should be read:

1. **Every individual statement is not slower as an `IN`**, and two of the three are materially
   faster. There is no read whose equality form is worth preserving.
2. **End to end the prototype's single-element arm is +1.4% against the pass-through control** — a
   real signed effect (26 of 36) but 1.4%. The statement table cannot account for it, since all three
   swapped statements are faster; the residual therefore lives in the prototype's own JavaScript (it
   rebuilds the id-set JSON per call where the repository binds a bare string) rather than in the
   predicate. **It is not attributed, and §8 says so.**
3. **1.4% is inside this instrument's resolution.** `narrow-path-regression.md` §5 states the same
   instrument "cannot detect a regression below about 5%", and phase 0's committed tolerance for the
   narrow path is **+15%**.
4. Adopting a second query shape to chase a 1.4% residual that no statement measurement reproduces is
   exactly the drift D6 exists to avoid. **If phase 4's real implementation shows an end-to-end cost,
   task 0.2's phase-4 re-run of `narrow-path-e2e.mjs` is where it is caught**, against a tolerance that
   was committed before any after-number existed.

**And the correctness half is settled independently of the clock:** `shadow-1` returned **byte-identical
ids to the shipped read on all 1 440 compared pages**, every one non-empty, at three magnitudes and
from two homes.

**The lexical branch widens far more cheaply than the dense one** — 1.24–1.28× to read every project
from the dominant home, against 1.69× for the dense read at the same corpus — because `LIMIT
rankWindowSize` is a TOTAL there, not a per-partition `k`. Widening does not make the branch fetch more
rows; it makes it reject fewer while scanning the same FTS index. From a thin home it costs more
(1.75–1.93×) for the same reason it costs the dense branch more: the added rows dwarf the home's.

**Query plans, recorded so a later audit does not mistake them for evidence.** The dense arms print the
identical opaque string, exactly as `vec-partition-capability.md` §4 recorded, so EQP still does not
discriminate:

- `dense eq` → `SCAN memory_vec VIRTUAL TABLE INDEX 0:3{___}___]Aa_&Aa_` + `USE TEMP B-TREE FOR ORDER BY`
- `dense IN(1) bound` → identical, both lines
- `lexical eq` → `SCAN memory_fts VIRTUAL TABLE INDEX 32:M3` + `SEARCH m USING INTEGER PRIMARY KEY (rowid=?)`
- `lexical IN(all) json_each` → the same two, plus `LIST SUBQUERY 1` + `SCAN json_each VIRTUAL TABLE INDEX 1:` + `CREATE BLOOM FILTER`

| list length | bound parameters           | `json_each` |
| ----------: | -------------------------- | ----------- |

share it, so the comparison BETWEEN them is untouched; no absolute figure in this sub-table may be
compared with any other table here.

## 7. Task 2.8 — the rank window for a widened query, decided

`computeRankWindowSize(limit, offset)` returns **64** for the default page. `k` applies **per named
partition**, so with the window kept the dense branch hands fusion up to `64 × N` rows while the
lexical branch's `LIMIT` stays a total of 64. The two candidate policies are: keep the window (the pool
grows with N), or divide it by N (the pool is constant and each project contributes fewer candidates).

Both were implemented and measured. The decisive table is not the clock — it is what each policy hands
to fusion. Untimed census over the same 40 queries, dense branch only:

| corpus / home      | arm                | k per partition | dense candidates / query | home share | per project                                                     |
| ------------------ | ------------------ | --------------: | -----------------------: | ---------: | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1 000 `vol-0`      | window kept, all 7 |              64 |                      272 |      23.5% | vol-2 64, vol-0 64, vol-1 64, vol-3 40, vol-4 24, vol-shared 16 |
| 1 000 `vol-0`      | window ÷ N, all 7  |              10 |                       60 |      16.7% | vol-shared 10, vol-3 10, vol-2 10, vol-0 10, vol-4 10, vol-1 10 |

| 1 000 `vol-shared` | window ÷ N, all 7  |              10 |                       60 |      16.7% | all six at 10                                                   |

**Decision — keep the window. The divided window is a per-project quota, which is the failure D6
already rejects, now measured rather than argued.** Under `k ÷ N` every project contributes exactly
the same number of dense candidates regardless of its size or its distance to the query: at 1 000
memories, `vol-shared` — which the kept-window arm shows can only offer **16** rows in total —
contributes **10** candidates, and `vol-0`, with 600 rows, also contributes **10**. That is the
proposal's own "a project holding three memories
yields a rank-1 row" in a different dress, and it makes the home project's share of the pool a
function of **how many projects the token happens to reach** (5.9% → 16.7% for `vol-shared` merely by
changing policy) rather than of the corpus. D6's own words: "It makes the result depend on how many
projects the token happens to reach, so the same query returns different rows after an unrelated
project is created."

**The number the decision costs**, from §4's `-divided` rows, is the end-to-end price of keeping the
window:

| corpus / home       | window kept (median p50) | window ÷ N | cost of keeping |
| ------------------- | -----------------------: | ---------: | --------------: |
| 1 000 `vol-0`       |                 10.44 ms |    9.50 ms |       **+9.9%** |
| 1 000 `vol-shared`  |                 10.00 ms |    9.17 ms |           +9.1% |
| 20 000 `vol-0`      |                 34.87 ms |   33.32 ms |           +4.7% |
| 20 000 `vol-shared` |                 35.92 ms |   33.60 ms |           +6.9% |
| 50 000 `vol-0`      |                 90.55 ms |   87.95 ms |           +3.0% |
| 50 000 `vol-shared` |                 84.27 ms |   81.04 ms |           +4.0% |

**Keeping the window costs 3.0–9.9% end to end, and the cost falls as the corpus grows.** That is the
price of not letting the number of authorized projects decide how many candidates the home project
gets, and it is worth paying.

**What this does NOT decide, and it is the half task 2.8 asked for: recall.** The task says "measure
both against recall on phase 3's corpus". Phase 3's corpus does not exist — it is being built
concurrently — and the volumetric corpus's vectors are pseudo-random, so ANY recall or page-membership
reading taken here would be a retrieval-quality claim the corpus explicitly cannot support (§2). The
decision above therefore rests on the pool-composition census (a structural property of `k` per
partition, independent of vector semantics) and on the measured latency, **not** on recall. **The
recall check is owed and is deferred to phase 4**, once phase 3's widened queries and cross-project
distractors exist; it is the natural companion of task 4.13.

## 9. What this does NOT establish

1. **It does not measure the shipped widening, because there is not one yet.** Instrument I3 is a
   prototype: the same service, the same `hybridSearch`, the same fusion, gate, boost and hydration,
   with three repository reads overlaid. Its fidelity is pinned by the id control (identical on all
   1 440 pages) and its harness cost by the pass-through arm (1.00×), but phase 4 must re-measure with
   the real implementation. Task 0.2's phase-4 re-run is the place, and it must be **paired and
   interleaved** against a worktree at the pre-phase-4 commit, per `narrow-path-regression.md` §6.
2. **The +1.4% end-to-end residual on the single-element arm is unattributed.** All three swapped
   statements are individually faster; the residual is therefore in the prototype's own JavaScript, but
   that is an inference, not a measurement. If it survives into phase 4 it will show up against phase
   0's committed +15% tolerance.
3. **No quality claim of any kind.** Synthetic vectors (§2). Page composition, foreign-row counts and
   the `-divided` arms' page differences appear in the raw JSON and are NOT read as retrieval
   evidence anywhere in this document. `pnpm run eval` is the instrument for quality and phase 3 is
   where it gains the power to answer this feature's question at all.
4. **The rank-window decision is made on pool composition and latency, not on recall** (§7). The recall
   half is owed to phase 4.
5. **One corpus shape, one skew.** 60/20/10/5/3/2 over six projects. A corpus with two equal large
   projects, or with fifty projects, would move §4's ratios. The `IN`-list measurement (§6) bounds the
   fifty-project direction for the STATEMENT but not for the end-to-end call.
6. **The empty authorized project is not isolated.** The corpus carries a `default` project with zero
   memories and the all-projects arms name it, but no arm differs from another only by its presence,
   so this document does not measure what design OQ1 costs. It remains an open question with a
   recorded default.
7. **One machine, one day, with a second agent running on it.** Between-arm comparisons are protected
   by interleaving inside one process; absolute milliseconds are not, and should not be compared with
   `narrow-path-regression.md`'s absolute figures even though the instrument is the same.
8. **It says nothing about concurrency.** One search at a time, one connection, no writer.
9. **Two edits are owed to `vec-partition-capability.md` and design D6**, and are named here rather
   than made silently in someone else's artifact: the 20 000-vector `IN (all 8)` cell is 8.13×, not
   12.8× (§8), and the predicate-free arm is not "never faster than `IN (all 8)`" — it is measurably
   faster, and is rejected because it carries no scope predicate rather than because it is slow.
