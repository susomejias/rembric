## Why

The entity channel's rarity gate measures its proportion over the wrong population, and the mismatch switches the channel off for exactly the memories it exists to serve.

The gate is `linkCount / scopeMemoryCount > ENTITY_RARITY_THRESHOLD → skip this entity` (`services/save-time-candidates.ts:176-183`). Both quantities are read from `db/repositories/entities-repository.ts`, and both use `sql\`${memory.status} != 'archived'\``— which admits`superseded`:

| method                                             | declares                                                            | implements      |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------- |
| `scopeActiveMemoryCount` (`:234`, pred `:242`)     | its **name**; docstring "the scope's total **active**-memory count" | `!= 'archived'` |
| `entityLinkCount` (`:255`, pred `:266`)            | docstring "Current **active**-memory link count"                    | `!= 'archived'` |
| `findOtherMemoriesForEntity` (`:285`, pred `:298`) | docstring "Other **active** memories"                               | `= 'active'` ✅ |

Three sites, three docstrings saying `active`, one predicate that agrees. The third was corrected on 2026-07-29 because it contradicted `memory-entities/spec.md:348` word for word ("an existing **active** memory"); the other two were left because no scenario made the converse testable. This is one copy-paste error fixed in one of three places, not a considered design.

**The consequence is that the gate blocks candidates it is structurally incapable of receiving.** The candidate set is now `active`-only, so an entity linked to 52 `superseded` rows plus 1 `active` row can contribute **exactly one** candidate. Against 100 active rows the gate computes `53/152 = 0.349` versus `ENTITY_RARITY_THRESHOLD = 0.15` and discards it. On the population candidates are actually drawn from it is `1/100 = 0.01`, admitted with two decimal orders to spare.

**And the failure concentrates on the evolving topic.** Superseded rows accumulate through `topic_key` upserts, permanently: `memory/spec.md:625-631` permits physical purge only for rows that are `archived` **and** unreferenced as a `predecessor_id`, and a superseded row is by construction referenced by its successor. So a long-lived topic's rows are never purged, and they all carry the _same_ entities — the same path, the same error code, the same commit. A 52-save topic chain about `migrate.ts` is 52 superseded links on `migrate.ts`, which is the exact shape of the arithmetic above. The channel silently disables itself on the topic it is most useful on, and the disabling gets worse the longer the topic lives.

**The gate's own charter says the measurement must match the candidate population.** `2026-07-25-add-entity-index/design.md` D6 records why the gate exists: a common entity "would flood the per-save candidate budget, **starving the lexical and dense channels**". Volume is not the live risk — `CANDIDATES_PER_SAVE_MAX = 5` bounds it, and `surface-save-candidate-total` measured the cap already binding on **38 of 38** saves (mean 11.2 detected, 56% dropped). What the cap does _not_ bound is composition: `save-time-candidates.ts:225-233` makes entity-sourced candidates lead unconditionally, so five entity candidates take all five slots. The gate is therefore the only thing allocating slots between channels — and only rows that can _occupy_ a slot can distort that allocation. Superseded links cannot. They inflate a measurement of starvation risk with rows incapable of causing it.

The same design left the threshold's population as an explicit open question ("A fixed link count, or a proportion of the scope's memories?"), and `0.15` has never had a covering test or a committed calibration. So there is no tuned number being disturbed here — there is a proportion whose denominator was never pinned.

Finally, the published contract already leans the right way and still could not catch this. `memory/spec.md:1119` defines the constant as "the maximum share of a scope's **active** memories an entity may be linked to"; `memory-entities/spec.md:364`'s scenario says "a large share of the scope's **active** memories". Both readings are one-directional — non-archived counting still satisfies "a very common entity generates no candidates", because active rows are counted on both sides. Nothing published says a **mostly-superseded** entity MUST still propose. And `entities-repository.test.ts:226` is titled "count active links and the scope total, excluding archived rows" over a fixture of two active rows and one archived one: no superseded row anywhere, so the test cannot fail. Three docstrings, two spec sentences and one test all said `active`; none of them was a guard.

## What Changes

- **Both rarity-gate reads move to `eq(memory.status, 'active')`**, matching the third method and making the two existing names and docstrings true rather than requiring them to be reworded. That the fix removes a contradiction instead of introducing new prose is the evidence, not decoration: `scopeActiveMemoryCount` becomes a method that counts active memories.
- **This is behavioural, not a compliance patch, and it is bidirectional — which is the part that must not be misstated.** "More entities pass" is false as a blanket claim. Writing `A` for active and `S` for superseded rows in scope, the ratio moves from `(Lₐ+Lₛ)/(A+S)` to `Lₐ/A`. For an entity carrying **no** superseded links the denominator shrinks with the numerator unchanged, so the ratio _rises_ by `(A+S)/A` and the gate gets **stricter**. On the production-shaped corpus this repo smoke-tested against (18 active / 17 superseded) that factor is **1.94**: an entity on 20 of 99 active rows in a scope with 99 superseded rows currently scores `20/198 = 0.101` and passes, and would score `20/99 = 0.202` and be blocked. That tightening is the intended semantics — an address on a fifth of the retrievable corpus genuinely is common — but it means the net effect on candidate volume is **unknown until measured**, in both directions, which is why the measurement below is a task and not a footnote.
- **Displacement, stated plainly, because it is the crux of whether this is a net good.** Entity candidates lead the merged list, and the response is `all.slice(0, perSaveMax)`. Where the cap already binds — 38 of 38 saves on the eval corpus, mean 11.2 detected against 5 kept — an additional admitted entity does **not** add a pending judgment: it _displaces_ a lexical or dense candidate that would otherwise have taken the slot. Pending-judgment volume is unchanged there, so this change does not worsen the attention budget that `surface-save-candidate-total` exists to address (this instance's queue reached 52; `order-relation-annotations` measured 1154 of 1164 annotations `pending_conflict` on an undrained corpus). Only on a scope sparse enough that fewer than five candidates are found does an admitted entity add a row, and a sparse scope is where an extra candidate is cheapest. The displacement is judged **favourable**: what enters is a pair sharing an exact identifier that no text or vector query connects, which is the case the channel was built for; what leaves is the fifth-ranked lexical or dense neighbour, which `memory.search` re-derives on demand at any time.
- **`ENTITY_RARITY_THRESHOLD` stays at `0.15`. Recalibration is a follow-up, deliberately, and named as such rather than dropped.** Two reasons. `0.15` was never calibrated against anything (the open question above; no covering test), so there is nothing to recalibrate _from_ — the honest sequence is to fix the population, measure the corrected distribution, and then calibrate against it. And moving the number in the same change confounds the two effects: a shift in candidate composition could no longer be attributed to the population fix, which is the one claim this change has to prove.
- **`memory-entities`' channel requirement states the population normatively, on both sides of the ratio, with a scenario for the converse.** The prose at `:350` ("a large share of the scope's memories") loses its ambiguity, and its "would flood the per-save candidate budget" clause is corrected to what is actually true post-cap: the cap bounds volume, the gate allocates slots. A new scenario pins the direction nothing published covers — an entity whose links are overwhelmingly superseded SHALL still propose — so the drift cannot be reintroduced by a predicate that satisfies only the existing one-directional scenario.
- **Rejected: a grep invariant in `test/invariants.test.ts`.** It would pin the current SQL text, and `tune-hot-query-paths` task 4.5 plans to replace `scopeActiveMemoryCount`'s query with a cached value or a maintained counter — at which point the grep either goes stale or forbids the optimisation. The population is pinned instead by the normative spec clause plus behavioural repository and service tests, which survive that rewrite and would fail a counter maintained over the wrong population. The invariants suite is not weakened or touched.
- **No `memory` requirement changes.** `memory/spec.md:1119` already reads "a scope's active memories" and its plain reading pins the numerator too; republishing that forty-line constants requirement to add a clause it already implies is churn with transcription risk. Verified by reading it, not assumed.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

_None._

### Modified Capabilities

- `memory-entities`: the save-time conflict-detection channel requirement gains a normative statement of the rarity gate's population (numerator and denominator both counted over `active` memories only), a corrected account of what the gate protects (slot composition, not volume — the per-save cap bounds volume), and a scenario requiring a mostly-superseded entity to still propose candidates.

## Impact

- `apps/server/src/db/repositories/entities-repository.ts` — the two predicates at `:242` (`scopeActiveMemoryCount`) and `:266` (`entityLinkCount`). Two lines. No other method changes; `findMemoriesByEntity` (`:164`) keeps `!= 'archived'` deliberately — the entity _retrieval_ path is specified as complete within scope, a different contract.
- `apps/server/src/db/repositories/entities-repository.test.ts` — the existing `scopeActiveMemoryCount and entityLinkCount` block (`:225-259`) has no superseded row in either fixture and therefore cannot detect the defect.
- `apps/server/src/services/save-time-candidates.test.ts` — the gate's behaviour through the service path; `ENTITY_RARITY_THRESHOLD` currently has no covering test at all.
- `openspec/specs/memory-entities/spec.md` — the "Entity overlap MUST be a save-time conflict-detection channel" requirement (`:346-378`).
- **No schema change, no migration, no derived-data invalidation.** The three entity tables' _contents_ are untouched; only a read predicate moves. First boot after upgrade behaves identically until the next `memory.save`, and rollback is a two-line revert with nothing persisted that records which population was used.
- **Invariants touched:** none violated. Append-only is untouched (no write path changes). Scope stays resolved at the service layer and passed into both scoped repository reads. `topic_key` convergence is not modified — this change is a _consequence_ of it, since the superseded accumulation the gate mismeasures is what convergence produces. Fresh-context judgment is preserved: candidate volume is unchanged wherever the per-save cap binds.
- **Coordination:** `tune-hot-query-paths` task 4.5 reworks `scopeActiveMemoryCount` for cost. This change must land first — see `design.md` D4.
